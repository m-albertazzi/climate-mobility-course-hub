About the course

Why this course

This online course explores the labour dimensions of the complex intersection of climate change and human mobility.

It looks at the impacts on labour markets, decent work and employment, at just transitions towards environmentally sustainable economies, and at labour mobility. With its unique tripartite structure bringing together governments, employers’ and workers’ organizations, the ILO is well placed to promote a holistic approach to these challenges.

The course does not give preference to migration over local adaptation: it presents labour mobility as one tool in the climate adaptation toolbox. It acknowledges that people already move, and will continue to do so, for different reasons in the context of climate change, and explores how labour market and employment strategies — including labour mobility — can help countries of origin and destination reduce vulnerabilities, build resilience, increase protection and benefit from the opportunities.

Objectives

What you will gain

By the end of the course, you will have…

  1. 1

    Explored the dynamics and complex interactions between climate change and human and labour mobility, including its gender dimensions.

  2. 2

    Analysed the impacts, and range of potential outcomes, of human mobility in the context of climate change on employment and labour market access, and the implications for decent work.

  3. 3

    Understood the scope of international and regional human rights frameworks, the Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work and other international labour standards, and the ILO’s Just Transition Guidelines in relation to climate-related human mobility.

  4. 4

    Established the importance of resilience, mitigation and adaptation strategies, notably relating to green jobs and skills.

…and you will be able to:

  1. 1

    Promote rights-based strategies, policies and tools to facilitate labour market access and employability of people vulnerable to climate change impacts, supporting social cohesion and improved living and working conditions of host and migrant or displaced populations.

  2. 2

    Analyse international legal and policy frameworks regulating access to employment and decent work in this context, including free movement agreements and bilateral labour migration agreements.

  3. 3

    Facilitate the roles that governments, workers’ and employers’ organizations, international organizations, civil society, private sector employers, women, and migrant and displaced populations themselves can play in facilitating access to decent work.

  4. 4

    Advocate for holistic approaches to inclusive labour markets for people affected by climate change that ensure their beneficial contribution to social and economic development.

Learning approach

How the course works

The course consists of an introduction and five online modules offered through the ITCILO e-Campus online platform, to be completed over six weeks for an estimated 60 learning hours. It is broken down into three phases.

  1. Phase 1 · Asynchronous

    Flexible learning

    Self-guided online learning on e-Campus, forum of discussion facilitated by experts, and assessment throughout the different phases of the course.

  2. Phase 2 · Synchronous

    “Real time” learning

    Live interactive sessions and engaging video presentations by highly experienced trainers, blended with individual and collaborative group exercises, peer-to-peer assessment and online technical forums on e-Campus.

  3. Phase 3 · Asynchronous

    End of course assignment

    An individual assignment applying the teachings acquired during the course.

  • Global perspective

    An action-oriented, highly participative approach, with particular attention to sharing international experiences with a view to their adaptation and practical application, including “good practices” presented through case studies.

  • Integrated e-learning

    An innovative e-learning experience combining self-paced modules, webinars and discussions, case studies, forum debates, role-play exercises and group work.

  • Peer-to-peer learning

    Practitioners from different parts of the world and different backgrounds join the e-learning to share their concrete experience with participants.

Participation and assessment

Course requirements

The process is organised on a scale of 0 to 100 points and requires the successful completion of a number of activities. Each activity is assigned a specific value, and the activities are grouped into the components below.

The training team reserves the right to award up to 10 extra points for exceptional contributions / active participation throughout the course.

The components

20 points

Weekly forum

Each week, we will post one or more discussion prompts in the weekly forum. Your weekly contribution to the forums of each module is mandatory and counts for a total of maximum 20 points (5 forums × 4 points).

Deadline: 6 December 2026.

30 points

Weekly quiz

At the end of each week, you need to complete a quiz of 10 multiple-choice questions. Correct answers earn 0.5 points and incorrect answers earn 0 points. Submission of the quizzes is mandatory and counts for a total of maximum 30 points (each weekly quiz = 5 points; 6 quizzes).

Deadline: 6 December 2026.

20 points

Group work

Active participation in the group work counts for a total of 20 points. The group work is based on three country case studies (Zimbabwe, Barbuda, Tuvalu), made available on the e-Campus with guiding questions. Groups present in Session 12 on 3 December 2026: up to 15 minutes per group, followed by 10 minutes of discussion and feedback.

30 points

Final assignment

If you wish to obtain a Certificate of Achievement, at the end of the course you will be asked to write a final assignment, which will count for up to 30 points.

Deadline: 18 December 2026.

Certificates

Certificate of Participation

You can stop after the weekly forum, weekly quiz and group work, and receive a Certificate of Participation.

Certificate of Achievement

To obtain a Certificate of Achievement, the passing mark is 60 out of 100 points + the final assignment (up to 30 points; deadline 18 December 2026).
